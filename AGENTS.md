# development

- static browser game with vendored three.js r160 in `vendor/three/`; no runtime build step.
- run `python3 devserver.py` from this folder for the no-cache server on port 5179.
- check changed javascript with `node --check <file>` and run `git diff --check`.
- verify in a browser. local-only `window.__rawah` hooks (`begin`, `goto`, `teleport`, `setHour`, `look`, `press`, `hold`, `step`, `map`, `travel(name)`, `weather(regime)`) drive deterministic checks when pointer lock is unavailable; `step(n)` only renders the last frame. keep the hooks disabled on public hosts.
- world data in `data/` is generated by `tools/bake_world.py` from real sources (USGS lidar, Sentinel-2, Overture/OpenStreetMap); edit the script and re-bake rather than hand-editing `world.json` or the binaries. downloads and expensive rasters cache in `tools/.cache/`.
- `World.height()` is lidar (`baseHeight`, quad split along the (c, r)-(c+1, r+1) diagonal) plus micro-relief from `src/detail.js`; the CDLOD vertex shader computes the same thing. keep the JS and GLSL halves of `detail.js` in lock-step (same hashing, octaves, offsets, weights) or the hiker will float or sink.
- `World.cover()` turns the baked Sentinel-2 land cover (live conifer, burned, aspen, rock) plus terrain into the weights flora uses; keep tree placement driven by it so trees match the satellite colour underneath.
- names come only from OpenStreetMap data; do not invent names for unnamed lakes or features.
- all sound is synthesized in `src/audio.js`; no audio files.
- trees are generated at startup in `src/trees.js` (painted atlas, card meshes, impostor bake); flora placement stays in `src/flora.js`. species: lodgepole below a line that moves with aspect and drainage (about 2900-3100 m), then spruce and subalpine fir (fir share rises with elevation), aspen from the satellite, krummholz at treeline. saplings (scale under `SAPLING`) are drawn only close up and are never impostors. impostor atlas has 32 cells; keep the variant count under that.
- rock is `src/rocks.js` (three shapes, hex colours are sRGB, vertex colours linear) placed by `Flora.#populate`: scatter, outcrops, tors above treeline, talus fans under steep rock, erratics. formations are built from the neighbouring cells too, and each tile keeps only the rocks inside it, so they stay seamless; keep that if you change them. big blocks go in `solids` so you can't walk through them. the near-ground pebble shader (`blocky` in `src/terrain.js`) is separate.
- camera feel lives in `src/camera.js` and is driven by `Hiker.phase` (PI per footstep); keep footstep audio and head bob on that same phase. `V` toggles first/third person; `src/avatar.js` is a placeholder meant to be replaced by a rigged model.
- the sky draws clouds as a five-slice slab (`slab()` in `src/sky.js`): lower slices wide and dark, upper ones shrinking to billowing tops, each looking toward the sun for self-shadow, plus a thin cirrus layer; slice 0's footprint matches `cloudDensity`, so the ground shadows still line up. keep `CLOUD_GLSL` and its JS twin `cloudDensityAt` in step if the density function changes.
- weather is a continuous function of time, `sampleWeather` in `src/weather.js` (slow systems, afternoon heating and instability, drifting cloud banks, dawn fog), seeded per game and tilted by the season's climate in `calendar.js`; `daySchedule`/`forecast` for the journal are read off the same function, and the named `REGIMES` are only what the settings menu can hold. the colour grade (vibrance, S-curve, split tone) is folded into the ACES tone mapper at the top of `main.js` via `THREE.ShaderChunk.tonemapping_pars_fragment`; change it there. weather state is a few numbers (cloud, thick, precip, fog, wind, temp, storm) eased toward the day's seeded schedule; everything else reads them. cloud density has one GLSL source (`CLOUD_GLSL` in `src/atmosphere.js`) used by the sky and the ground's cloud shadows, plus a two-octave JS twin (`cloudDensityAt`); keep them in step.
- materials join the shared light and weather through `withAtmosphere(shader, { translucency, weather })`; don't fork per-material fog, cloud shadows, wetness or snow.
- gamepad input uses the standard mapping (what an Xbox controller reports). game actions go through the same key codes as the keyboard (`PAD` in `src/input.js`); menus and the map take named buttons via `Input.onPad`.
- the date lives in `src/calendar.js`; seasonal looks (leaves, grass curing, snowpack, lake ice, meadow green-up) come from it, not from hard-coded months elsewhere. the season is a setting and changing it reloads, because the tree atlas and impostors are painted for the season at boot.
- UI type: Gabarito for interface text, Paytone One for names and titles (vendored in `fonts/`; canvases use the same two, and `main.js` waits for them before painting). the logo is `assets/brand/rawah.png` (white wordmark) and the favicon `assets/brand/icon.png`. don't add other fonts.
- the map and field guide are one window (`src/map.js`, `#map` in index.html): the map is the satellite picture tinted navy with peach trails (`build`), places are markers you click or tap to get a card, and the list only appears for search or a filter. the field guide tab (`#guide`) shows lakes and summits from `state.discovered`, the wildlife, flora, checkpoints and easter eggs from `src/guide.js`. `guide.js` checks what the player stands in twice a second (`feedGuide` in main.js) and rolls sightings with a per-entry `chance`; keep the entry facts (`cover`, `dusk`, `alpine`...) in step with what `feedGuide` builds. mouse, touch, keyboard and pad all go through the same selection.
- the player body is `src/character.js`: a read-only view of `Hiker` (mode, stance, action, speed) that picks clips from `src/anims.js` and crossfades them. clips are Mixamo FBX in `assets/characters/anims/`, loaded lazily (`CORE` before the model shows, `EXTRA` after), with hip x/z travel stripped at load so the controller owns movement. characters face +z. the GLB export has no metalness, so `character.js` forces it to 0 (glTF defaults to fully metal, which renders black). `?char=grant` picks the other model. add moves by adding the clip to `anims.js`, a state to `Hiker`, and a branch in `Character.#want`.
- the other two friends are `src/companions.js` (`Party`): each is a `Character` posed from a `Body` that mimics the Hiker fields it reads (`face` and `emote` let a script turn them or hold a clip). they follow you, and every 30-70 s the pair walks a few metres off and chats, sits, or play-fights (punches plus hit reactions). they share the player's `AnimLibrary`. whoever you are not playing as is a companion (`?char=` picks the player).
- character GLBs: `tools/characters/convert.html` + `convert.mjs` load a Mixamo-rigged FBX with three's FBXLoader (serve a folder holding `<name>.fbx`, `<name>_dl.jpg` and a three checkout; run `node convert.mjs blaize grant corey` with playwright-core), merge vertices, stand the Z-up rig up (root rotation -90 degrees about X, scale 0.01) and export with GLTFExporter. The rigs come out Z-up with their own bone frames while the animation clips are Y-up Mixamo, so `Character` re-fits every clip to its rig (`retarget` in `src/anims.js`: each bone's turn from rest in the world, carried onto the rig's rest; hips height scaled). Without that the body twists or lies down. `RAWAH_RETARGET=false` turns it off for comparison. the top joint Mixamo shows over the head is a leaf end bone and does nothing.
- characters are lit by `skinLight` in `character.js`: shared fog, cloud shadow and sky occlusion via `withAtmosphere`, a sky-tinted rim, and baked texture highlights eased down. keep new character materials going through it.
- fighting lives in `Hiker` (`ACTIONS`, `#combat`, `receiveHit`): punches emit `strike` events with a direction and reach for whatever ends up doing hit detection; nothing in the world takes hits yet.
- the three loaders in `vendor/three/examples/jsm/` (GLTF, FBX with NURBS and fflate) are copied unmodified from three r160.
- grass remembers the hiker in a 40-slot ring of pressed capsules (`#trample` in `src/grass.js`, `uTrA`/`uTrB`, shared with `atmosphere.js` for snow prints). the ring is not looped over per grass vertex: `PressMap` draws it once a frame into a 256x256 texture over 64 m around the camera (G/B lean, A press; a pure function of the ring and the clock), and the grass vertex shader reads it with one fetch. `grass.update` takes the body heading from `avatar.heading` and the renderer (for that pass). lying and sitting renew their patch every frame so it stays flat under you; everything else springs back (`HOLD`, `SPRING`). keep the slot count in the JS and the press shader loop in step (both read `TRAMPLE`).
- grass clumps are instanced on a camera-following cell grid in 6x6 blocks, and the vertex shader leaves at once (`gl_Position` off screen) for any clump that will not be drawn: cells outside the block, outside the radius, thinned by the detail ring, or where the cover/mask say nothing grows (the altitude test comes after and can only lower growth, so testing cover first is safe). detail rings (`RING_A`, `RING_B` fractions of the radius): blocks within the first draw every cell as three-card clumps, the next ring every 2nd cell and the outer every 4th as two-card clumps, cells aligned on the world grid, wider (`wm`) and faded (`lodW`) across a few metres so the thinning does not pop. a block's ring is fixed because the grid follows the camera; `aStride` carries it. measured 24.6 -> 13.8 ms (log depth on) and 11.2 -> 7.8 ms (off) at 1600x1000.
- leaving deep water after a real swim starts the `climbOut` action in `Hiker` (played by `Character` from `Swimming To Edge`, with a vertical offset easing from the waterline up to the bank); `exitCool` stops it repeating when you bob at the edge.
- tools, felling and loose rocks are `src/props.js`. flora tiles stay placement data: anything you disturb goes through `Flora.find`/`take` (removed from the tile's items and solids, remembered by `itemKey` in `flora.removed`, which `#populate` honours when a tile is rebuilt) and then lives in props as a falling or lying tree (a one-instance copy of the species mesh from `flora.treeMesh`), a stump, or a loose rock (`rockParts` plus `rockPose`). `props.save()`/`load()` ride along in the main save; `reset()` is a new game. rock weight is by scale: under `CARRY_MAX` you can lift it, up to `PUSH_MAX` you shove it (`Props.collide`, called from the end of `Flora.collide` and only for the hiker's own position), beyond that it is bedrock. rocks stacked on another (placed off `restY`) stay put. an axe swing (`chop` in `ACTIONS`) is 0.62 s and a mature tree takes `TREE_HITS` (6) axe strokes in `props.js`: three into a wedge on your right and three into one on your left, then it falls. the first stroke takes the tree out of the forest (`flora.take`) and puts a one-instance copy in `props.cutting` whose trunk prism is replaced by a finer one (`carvedTree` in `src/cuts.js`: 36 sides, a level floor and a sloping roof per notch, cut faces wearing the aspen bark strip); every stroke rebuilds it deeper, and when it falls the carved mesh splits at the notches (both floors share one height, `CUT_H`, 0.8 m): the trunk below stays as a stump with a flat pale top (`#makeStump`, saved in `stumpCuts`), and everything above, with a flat cut face under it, falls from that height, sliding off the stump as it tips (`lift` in `#poseTree`). half-cut trees are saved in `cuts`. `trees.js` records each trunk's shape in `geometry.userData.trunk` for this. the first-person swing is sideways only when felling a standing tree: `SIDE.R` from the right for the first three strokes and `SIDE.L`, mirrored in position with the head turned over so the edge still leads (it is not a plain reflection, which would put the edge behind the head), from the left for the rest (head flat, turned between poses as quaternions); sawing a lying trunk uses the overhead chop (`VM.axe`). about four seconds of holding the button.
- the hand tool is `tools.js` (metres, grip at the origin, head +Y, edge +X). in third person `Character.#gripOf` mounts it on `mixamorigRightHand` in the straightened rest frame (see below); in first person `Props` hangs it off the camera and animates it from the action's progress. a swing plays `Stable Sword Outward Slash` (the one swing clip; `ACTION_SPAN` in `character.js` plays 0.25-1.5 s of it so the blade lands at the action's hit time, 0.4 of `dur`, mid-sweep). a proper axe clip would replace it in `ACTION_CLIP`; re-time `hits` in `ACTIONS` to match.
- retargeting straightens the arm chain first (`straighten` in `src/anims.js`): the clips are authored on a straight T-pose, and a rig whose arms rest bent or lowered (Corey's forearms bend about 30 degrees, Blaize's about 25) would carry that bend into every pose. each arm bone's rest orientation is turned to point where the clips' bone points before the world-space delta is applied. when adding a character, check the arms: pose the rig with a retargeted clip and compare bone directions with the clip's own.
- distance: tree impostor range is `flora.radius` in `quality.js` (each tile costs about 4 ms to populate and every refill walks all of them, so go easy); past it the terrain's `ALBEDO` paints a canopy from the cover map (or, for the far mesh, from how dark the satellite pixel is); valley haze is `uValley` in the shared fog in `withAtmosphere`, driven by the hour in `main.js`.
- telekinesis is the fourth tool (`T`), `src/mind.js` over `src/sculpt.js`. the ground you reshape is a separate 1 m grid of height offsets, sparse (32 m chunks in a 32x32 atlas texture behind a 464x464 index texture), clamped to +-14 m (`LIMIT`) from the lidar. `editH` (in `SCULPT_GLSL`, pulled into `HEIGHT_GLSL`) adds it in the terrain and grass vertex shaders and to the terrain normal; `World.height` adds the JS twin `Sculpt.at`. keep those two in step, and the cell size and chunk size (hard-coded as 1 and 32 in the GLSL). `CDLODTerrain` pads its min/max boxes by `LIMIT`. when an area changes, `main.js` `shaped()` calls `flora.reground(rect)` (drops the tiles so trees and rocks are replanted) and `props.settle(rect)` (loose rocks fall or ride up). sculpt chunks are saved as centimetre Int16 base64 in the main save (`sculpt`). `Hiker.channel` while sculpting swaps the walk for the Carrying clip and slows you.
- the build: `Character.#bulk` scales the chest, shoulders and arms by world-space factors (`BULK`, with `BULK_SCALE` per character) after the rig is measured, so retargeting still sees the original skeleton; head, hands and legs keep their size so the feet keep the ground.
- UI style lives in `style.css`: two brand colours, peach `#f6d3bd` (accent, primary buttons, N on the compass) and navy `#37587b` (selection fill, toggles), on neutral black and white; `--navy-light` is the tint for sky, lakes and the grip meter. every size comes from the `--t-*` scale and nothing is smaller than `--t-xs` (12 px), canvas text included (`hud.js`, `map.js`). the controls reference (`renderControls` in `main.js`) shows only the scheme in use, with a switch for the others; keep it to the essentials. the wordmark is `assets/brand/rawah.png` (the outline logo, 1400 px wide).
- `Character.#reach` lifts both arms over whatever clip plays while `Hiker.channel` is set. `CDLODTerrain` replaces the stretched satellite colour with bare earth and bedding on steep sculpted faces (`editSlope`). `Props.settle` also moves stumps and felled trees with the ground.
- posture: `Character.#upright` turns the hips so the spine runs along world up (85% of the way), while standing or walking; the retargeted clips otherwise lean about 10 degrees forward in idle. lying, sitting, climbing, swimming and the flip keep their own lean (`UPRIGHT_SKIP`).
- footprints: `pressAt` in `atmosphere.js` reads the shared trample ring (`uTrA`/`uTrB`, shared with `grass.js`); in snow it lifts the snow where something has stood and darkens what is pressed. it is shading, not geometry, so the snow surface itself does not dip. snow buries grass tufts gradually (they shrink, and only the deepest vanish).
- the boxel field is `src/boxels.js`: a 512² certified-sleeping grid of moisture (`u`, R channel) and wear (`w`, G) the ground remembers — footsteps, tire tracks, fires drying it (`dry()`), rain saturating it (`wet()`). every cell carries a proven live window so only cells that can change get integrated; `#paint` re-renders the DataTexture only when a step or event dirtied it, throttled to ~12 Hz. the terrain albedo darkens wet and trails wear (`BOXEL_COLOR` in `src/terrain.js`); grass reads the same texture in its vertex shader — wear lies blades down for good, moisture reads lusher. `sample(x, z)` gives the CPU view (valley mist feeds on damp ground at dawn). keep `uBoxel` coords as `p / uExtent + 0.5` everywhere. overlapping events are composed as the paper says (Prop. 4.2, Thm. 6.7, Remark 4.3), not one full-tolerance window each: a per-cell ledger `L` sums every live event's certified peak (`#ledgerAdd`: `A s(rho)` capped by the event's own density, with mirror images for the reflecting edges), a cell sleeps while `L <= epsWin`, and `eps` is split three ways (`epsWin` sleeping cells, `epsRet` the decaying residual of retired events, `epsCut` dropped ledger tails). an event retires (`#retireAt`) when its whole field is under `rho0`, only while the retired residuals fit `epsRet`; past `nMax` live events the closest pair merge into one disc of summed mass. wear sleeps at the exact first passage of the cell's own value. `__rawah.benchRain()` runs a rain stream against a dense reference (the old per-event windows missed the tolerance by 100x and more; the ledger stays under it with zero violations). the lattice windows for k > 0 are still the validated continuum envelope, not the paper's proved diamond (`ell` in `boxels.js` is its decay length), so `stats.violations` is the audit.
- firewood is `src/wood.js`, on top of the axe felling in `props.js`: fell a tree ([T] axe, click) and it lies whole in `props.lying`; the axe on its thick end (`props.strike` hands it to `Wood.strike` when no standing tree is in reach) first takes the branches off (the lying instance is hidden and one bare trunk mesh wearing the atlas bark strip takes its place), then every two strokes saws a fire-log length off the cut end; each log flies to a pyramid stack beside the trunk. when the usable trunk is gone `props.dropLying` removes it. [U] at a stack takes an armful (pack of 16) or, at the camp woodpile, stacks the pack; [J] (or [K] away from the fire) drops the pack as a stack; [F] at the fire lays a log (+60 min), and lighting a fire costs three logs from the pack or camp pile. unfinished trunks and ground stacks are not saved (the tree comes back whole); the pack, the camp pile and the cut count are.
- coffee is `src/coffee.js`: [K] near a lit fire takes the three leaning logs off (`Fire.setTeepee`), sets a grate across it and puts a speckled-enamel percolator on it. [U] facing the pot starts a ~40 s brew, then pours six sips into the thermos tumbler; [O] sips (gamepad: ← when away from the fire, touch: Sip), adding stamina through `coffeeBoost` in `main.js` and a little warmth. the pot and thermos are saved.
- breakfast is `src/breakfast.js`: with the grate rigged, [K] again puts a cast-iron skillet of bacon and eggs on the grate's free end (child of `coffee.stand`); [U] facing it eats once cooked (perfect until `GOOD`, burnt past `BURNT`).
- the family tent is `src/famtent.js`, chosen in Settings → Tent (`settings.tent`, 'solo' or 'family') and pitched with [C] 5 m ahead (`camp.pitch(x, z, yaw, kind)`, `camp.campProblem` wants level, clear ground). `WALLS` are plan segments with doorways left open; `Hiker`, `ATV` and `Party` actors call `solids(p, r)` (`camp.collide`) and `floorAt`, wired in `main.js`. `camp.doorDist` is where scripted scenes use the door.
- a friend's calls of nature live in `Party.#business` (`companions.js`): every 5-9 minutes, when the pair is just following you, one friend picks a standing tree (`flora.treesAround`) and a spot on its far side from you, walks there, holds the `Crouch Idle 01` emote and calls `party.onDrop`; `src/dump.js` turns that into falling droppings, a small pile with flies and a buzz when you are near, shrinking away after ~200 s (not saved). dev: `__rawah.dump`, set `party.urge = 0` (after `party.endWork()`) to trigger one.
- keys added by the camp features: [U] is the one "use" key (sit, ride, take firewood, pour coffee, eat), [K] cook, [O] sip, [J] drop firewood; the gamepad's L3 is [K] (cook, or drop the pack away from the fire).
- fireworks are `src/fireworks.js`, the fifth tool ([T] cycles; `fireworks` in `tools.js`): [Click] sets a mortar tube down 1.1 m ahead (up to 36; [N] on a pad or touch, which also steps the style each time), [R] (or [K]) lights the one you are by, a tube within 2 m of a burning one catches from it a beat later so a row ripples, [I] changes the style (Peony, Chrysanthemum, Willow, Ring, Heart, Palm, Strobe, Rainbow, Saturn, Gay Bois, Penis, Spiral), [U] picks up unlit ones. a lit fuse shortens with a glowing tip spitting sparks (`Sparks`, CPU particles, ring buffer); after `FUSE_S` the tube thumps and a shell climbs 105-150 m on a decelerating path with a spark trail and a whistle, then breaks. a break is a few hundred stars written once into `Stars` (`TR` points per star: the star and its trail), and the vertex shader places every point from the break's birth time alone with drag and gravity in closed form (`DRAG`, `GRAV`, per-star multipliers `aPhys`), so a burst costs nothing per frame. flat shapes (heart, rings) face the player. three pooled point lights, made at start so shaders never recompile, flash the trees and ground; the boom and crackle arrive at the speed of sound (`delay` on the sound event). custom shaders here carry the `logdepthbuf` chunks. nothing is saved; `fireworks.clear()` on a new game. dev: `__rawah.fireworks`.
- `Gay Bois` is a words firework (`wordPoints` in `fireworks.js`): the text is drawn to a canvas in Paytone One, sampled on a 6 px grid, and each lit pixel becomes one star (no trail) that flies to its place in a plane tilted back to face the player's eyes square on (`facing(to, n3)`: their level right, and up the picture along the way to their eyes; hearts do the same), in pride-flag stripes by height. stars slow to rest at v/k, so the picture keeps its shape while it hangs and sinks slowly (`g` 0.07); the shell goes higher (`H`) so it reads from the ground. to spell something else change the lines passed to `wordPoints`.
- `Penis` is a cartoon doodle in pink stars (`doodle()`: a filled silhouette sampled on a grid, tilted 0.3 rad, shaded by part) in the same face-the-watcher plane as the words, then, 1.2 s later, 190 long-tailed white comets (`delay` on a star offsets its birth; `o` its start from the burst centre) spray from its tip up and across the sky. stars can carry `delay`, `o` (start offset) and `flash` (one point, no trail).
- field of view: the base is `settings.fov` (default 80, Settings -> Field of view, 60-110; the old saved default of 72 is bumped to 80), and `CameraRig` opens it up with speed: `SPRINT_FOV` (11 degrees) while sprinting on foot, and for the quad `fovBoost` from `main.js` (up to 16 degrees by speed, +4 more boosting), all scaled by `settings.fovfx` (Settings -> Widen with speed, 0-150%) and eased in and out.
- profiling: on a local host `main.js` accepts `?aa=0&logdepth=0&shadows=0&dpr=1&grass=30` to override the quality preset for A/B timing, and `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` gives per-frame GPU milliseconds (wrap a `renderer.render` in a query after `tick(dt, false)`, with `requestAnimationFrame` stubbed so the real loop does not share the GPU). measured on an M-series GPU at 1600x1000: the log depth buffer more than doubles frame time (`logDepth` makes every fragment write `gl_FragDepth`, which disables early depth rejection), grass is the biggest single item, and `flora.update` tile populates are the CPU hitches.
- depth: the renderer uses an ordinary depth buffer (near 0.4, far 40000) in every preset. `logDepth` in `quality.js` is a logarithmic buffer kept as an option (Settings -> Distance depth -> Extended, `settings.depth`, reloads; near 0.1, far 90000) because it writes every fragment's depth, which turns off early depth rejection and costs about 1.7x the frame time on an M-series GPU (13.9 vs 8.5 ms at 1600x1000, 20.4 vs 11.2 ms at 2048x1536). checked for flicker against the log buffer at the highest summit, the biggest lake and the trailhead (six views; camera nudged 2 cm between frames): terrain, trails and water are clean, only grass blades and tree edges change between frames, as parallax. things within 0.4 m of the eye are clipped, so keep held items and first-person props beyond that. `polygonOffset` works again with the standard buffer (trails use it).
- fire smoke and heat are `src/plume.js`, on the certified driven front of the paper (Example 6.8, Theorems 6.5-6.7): a 96 m, 1 m cell grid centred on the fire holds two `Field`s (smoke: D 1, k 0.03, wind up to 1.6 m/s; heat: D 2, k 0.15, wind 0.5), the fire is a driven source (cells held at `g(t) <= U`, flickering freely) and the wind is the prevailing direction scaled by `weather.wind`. a per-axis exponential supersolution for the upwind scheme (`rate()`: downwind at the strongest wind, upwind at no wind, k split between axes) bounds the field outside the source for every forcing and every wind speed, so cells it clears are exactly zero and asleep; sources add in one ledger (the fire and a burning skillet are certified together), a stopped source retires after `ln(U/rho0)/k` s within `epsRet`, edges are absorbing (which only lowers the solution, so no Remark 6.9 correction). `Field.bench()` (`__rawah.benchPlume()`) runs a flickering fire and a swinging wind against a dense run: error 6e-7 against 0.02, zero violations. uses: the warmth target is `22 * clamp(heat/0.2)` (`env.fireWarm`; warmer downwind), smoke over 0.12 at your position caps your stamina, coughs and toasts, the fire's smoke particles lean with the wind (`uDrift`), and 48 sprites draw the field.
- live water ripples are `src/waves.js`: a port of the wave solver in `vendor/threejs-water` (MIT, Evan Wallace's WebGL Water via jeantimex; notice in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`): a 256² half-/full-float ping-pong pair (R height, G velocity, B/A slope), a Hann-profile drop (`WaveField.drop(x, z, radius, strength)`, `line()` for wakes), a step pass at a wave speed set in m/s (two sub-steps per 1/60 s, absorbing rim) and a slope pass. the 38 m window scrolls with the hiker in whole cells (`#shift`, nothing blurs); a stream's current (`currentAt` in `main.js`, 0.55 m/s downhill along the nearest segment) advects the field; the solver sleeps once the biggest drop has decayed under `eps` (same certified-sleeping idea as the boxels). `WAVE`/`WAVE_GLSL` give lakes (`features.js` `buildLakes`) and streams (`buildStreams`) `waveSlope(xz)`, added to their normal perturbation next to the older ring slots. drops come from: footsteps and swimming strokes (`'step'` events on `'water'`), the splash on entering, rain (`weather.precip`, only where `waterAt`), the fishing bobber (`fishing.onRipple`), and a quad's wake through a creek (`rideStep`). pool optics and caustics from the vendor repo are not used. dev: `__rawah.waves`, `__rawah.waterAt(x, z)`.
- ambient water (`AMBIENT_GLSL`/`WATER` in `src/waves.js`, used by `buildLakes` and `buildStreams`): `windSea` (six wave trains around the wind with deep-water dispersion, noise-warped, gust patches riding downwind, size from `weather.wind`), `rainRings` (hashed-cell random rings in two sizes, density from `weather.precip`, replacing the old periodic sine grid), and for streams `flowDetail` (the normal map carried along the stream's own flow, `aFlow` vertex attribute, in two out-of-phase layers cross-faded, the second rotated and rescaled, so it runs without looping) plus a little white where the surface is busiest. the scrolled normal map is only fine detail now (`normalScale` 0.3 and 0.35). `main.js` sets `WATER.uWT / uWind / uRainAmt` each frame.
- the water remembers: `src/waterbox.js` is a second `Boxels` instance (512², about 29 m cells, `d` 0.3, `k` 0.012, foam fade 0.08, eps 0.01, run at half the ground's clock) holding R turbidity (silt: wading, swimming, a quad, a fish or a cast stirs it; heavy rain washes it into creeks; it spreads and settles) and G foam (whitecaps when `uWind` > 0.55, wakes, creek rain). streams carry silt as a cascade of smaller `wet` events that walk downstream (`carry`), not an advection term; the cascade is the only part that is not a plain boxel event. lakes, ponds and streams tint and foam from it through `waterState`/`waterTint` (`WATERBOX_GLSL`, sampled with linear filtering over `p / world.extent + 0.5`). keep `d`/`k` such that the decay length `ell` (about `1/acosh(1 + k/2d)`) is 5 cells or more: with a shorter one the continuum windows no longer bound the lattice (checked: 2 cells fails, 5 holds). cells are coarse next to a 2.4 m creek, so silt reads as a soft tint over a stretch of it, not a per-metre plume.
- boxel fixes found while doing the water: the step is now Jacobi (every cell reads the old values via `next`, so the result no longer depends on the order of the awake list; the dense reference in the benches is Jacobi too); a deposit that leaves a sleeping cell above `epsWin` wakes it, and a cell only sleeps once it holds no more than `epsWin`; merging two overlapping events adds their densities (a point deposit stacked on a point deposit was being capped at the larger one). `sShift` (constructor `shift`) is how far the continuum envelope is moved inward; it did not change any result here.
- lightning is `src/lightning.js`. the channel is grown on a 64³ voxel grid (`LeaderGrid`, 34 m by about 40 m voxels) by the dielectric breakdown model: the potential between the charge region (top, 0) and the ground (bottom, 1) solves a *screened* Laplace equation (screening length 220 m, space charge and ground ions; plain Laplace has no locality, a screened field decays like exp(-r/screen), which is what lets the update be local), each step the channel takes one voxel from its 18-neighbour candidates with probability proportional to the potential^eta (1.7 for ground strokes, 1.4 in-cloud), the new voxel becomes a conductor, and the potential is relaxed only in a window of R = 10 voxels around it; the window's rim is the certificate (`stats.edge`, `stats.violations` counts steps where it still moved by more than `tol` = 4e-3: a few percent, early steps near the cloud). kinds: `cg` (cloud to ground, 34 m cells), `ic` (in-cloud, 110 m) and `cc` (anvil crawler, 190 m). `buildBolt` turns the tree into a trunk (the path to the ground) plus forks (brightness by hops from the trunk, short twigs dropped), jittered off the grid and kinked; `BoltView` draws it as camera-facing ribbons in a shader (`DoubleSide`: the ribbon winds away from the camera, culling it hides everything; fog off; additive, HDR). timeline per strike (`#intensity`): a faint stepped leader (0.2-0.55 s) feeding down the tree by order, then 1-6 return strokes (1 ms rise, 16-66 ms decay, a continuing current under some, 35-125 ms apart), then a cooling afterglow. colour is physical (`blackbody`: about 28,000 K blue-white, cooling to amber; `transmit`: Rayleigh plus rain scattering by distance, so far bolts come out yellower and dimmer; hydrogen-alpha pink creeps into the halo in rain). each bolt lights the cloud deck in the sky shader (`uBoltDir`/`uBoltCol`, up to three), the global flash (`weather.flash`, merged each frame in `main.js`) and one point light at the strike. strikes scorch the ground (`boxels.dry` and `wear`), churn water (`waves`, `waterCells`) and thunder arrives at the speed of sound from the return stroke (`audio.thunder(dist, kind, strength)`). `Settings -> Weather -> Severe thunderstorm` (`REGIMES.severe`, also rare in the live weather as `severe` in `sampleWeather`) flashes every 1-4 s with some strikes within a kilometre. dev: `__rawah.lightning` (`strike({from, dist, azimuth, type})`, `active`, `grid.stats`).

(a thin base haze that thickens with rain, the `Mist` boxel field, the plume's smoke grid uploaded as a texture); in-scatter is Henyey-Greenstein (g 0.68, brightest toward the sun) in `uSunCol` (gold at low sun). `Mist` is a third `Boxels` instance (about 29 m cells, d 0.5, k 0.012): wet ground steams when the sun comes onto it (strongest in the cool morning), lakes smoke at dawn, heavy rain leaves a haze. nothing is post-processed (the scene tone maps in its own shaders), so the shaft light is added with `uGain` standing in for that curve; it needs shadows on (Settings -> Light shafts and Shadows). not done: UnrealBloomPass is not vendored (only curves, libs, loaders, utils of `examples/jsm`), a bloom/radial-blur chain would mean moving the whole frame through a render target and re-doing the tone map in an output pass; the cost of the slices has not been measured on a GPU.

the camera is set here (`state.intro` takes it from `CameraRig`, like `state.travel`), `focus` feeds flora streaming and the sun's shadow camera, the HUD is hidden, letterbox bars and the place name slide in (`#cine`), and any key or tap skips. `main.js` owns the people: `introExit(i, door)` puts you (from the car) and your two friends (from the car and the truck) at their doors on the road side, you walk out with `introStep` (like `cutStep`, through `hiker.carried`) and the friends with `party.pins` (a new actor -> target map in `Party.update`), and `introEnd()` puts everyone at the start spot, restores the view, the HUD and the parked cars, and shows the hello toasts. the vehicles' engine is the quad's `audio.engine`. tested at West Branch (aerial, cut, arrival, exit, handover, skip); the other three trailheads build their drive but were not watched.
